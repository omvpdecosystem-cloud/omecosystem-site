What is OM Ecosystem?

A single system for governing complex decisions. One scientific foundation — Onion Theory — carried into practice by three pillars: research that validates it, services that apply it, and AI that makes it usable. Each pillar stands on its own; together they are one ecosystem.

OFFICIAL POSITIONING STATEMENT

The Onion Model is a layered framework for Ecosystem Management and Ecosystem Governance that enables the coordination, propagation, validation, and explanation of decisions across complex socio-technical ecosystems.

OM-R&D is the research pillar of the OM Ecosystem. OM-VPD is the shared software platform of the OM Ecosystem.

One foundation. One platform. Three pillars.

HOW THE ECOSYSTEM IS BUILT

  1. FOUNDATION

    The theoretical and architectural basis

    Onion Theory · LEGA

    Onion Theory is the theory, the Onion Model is its framework and LEGA is its architecture. The foundation sits above the pillars and belongs to none of them.

  2. SHARED SOFTWARE PLATFORM

    The shared software and computational decision-support infrastructure

    OM-VPD

    Onion Model – Value Propagation Decision Platform

    Decision-support software that implements aspects of Onion Theory, following LEGA. The pillars share it; it is not a pillar itself.

    • OM-R&D analysis and visualization tool in research and papers
    • OM-LOS analytical basis for consulting services (when active)
    • OM-AI planned integration

THESIS

The Onion Model as a Managerial Decision-Making Framework: A Case Study of Danish and Turkish Housing Markets

Life Cycle Management (LCM) applies Life Cycle Assessment (LCA) results in sustainability-oriented decision-making. Much existing research remains focused on the product level and does not systematically explain the relationships between different levels of decision-making in complex socio-technical systems, leaving a gap between life cycle analysis and governance. To address this gap, this thesis proposes the Onion Model of Life Cycle Management as a managerial decision-making framework: a multi-layer architecture that connects life cycle evidence to the decision levels at which it is acted upon.
